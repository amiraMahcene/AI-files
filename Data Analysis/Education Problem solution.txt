 ✅ CLEANING STEPS
1. Fix Inconsistent Formats

    Convert words to numbers:
    "four" → 4, "five" → 5, "six" → 6, "fifteen" → 15, "ninety" → 90

    Standardize Yes/No:
    “YES”, “yes”, “y” → Yes
    “no”, “No” → No
2. Fill or Flag Missing Data

    Replace missing numerical values with N/A or estimate if logical.

    Mark text blanks clearly as "Unknown".
    
3. Convert All Numbers to Same Format

    Attendance: remove % or add it consistently.

    Sleep hours: convert “6 hrs”, “4h”, etc. to uniform format (6, 4, etc.).

4. Drop Irrelevant Columns

    Cartoon preference and Instagram followers are unrelated to math performance → we’ll remove them.

🧠 Step 5: Get Insights – What Improvements Can Be Made?

✅ 1. Promote Private Tutoring or Extra Support

    Insight: Students who receive private tutoring perform significantly better.

    Action:

        * Offer after-school support classes.

       * Set up peer tutoring programs or online help sessions.

✅ 2. Improve Student Attendance

    Insight: Students with good attendance (>80%) perform better.

    Action:

        * Raise awareness of attendance importance among students and parents.

       *  Track and follow up with students who have frequent absences.

✅ 3. Encourage Homework Completion

    Insight: Homework helps reinforce learning, and students who do it regularly get better grades.

    Action:

       * Monitor homework submissions.

       *  Reward consistent completion with positive points or class privileges.

✅ 4. Educate Students About Healthy Sleep Habits

    Insight: Students who sleep well (6–9 hours) do better.

    Action:

        Integrate mini-lessons about sleep and productivity.

        Involve parents in promoting healthier sleep schedules at home.

✅ 5. Involve Parents in the Learning Process

    Insight: Students whose parents are more educated often get higher grades, possibly due to better support at home.

    Action:

        Host parent workshops to teach simple strategies for helping children with math.

        Create take-home guides for parents, regardless of their education level.

✅ 6. Identify At-Risk Students Early

    Insight: Patterns in attendance, homework, and past grades can help identify students at risk of failing.

    Action:

        Use the data to build a “risk score.”

        Assign a mentor or counselor to follow up with those students.

By analyzing students’ habits and conditions, we identified six key improvement strategies:

    Support struggling students through tutoring and extra help.

    Encourage regular attendance, as it’s linked to better grades.

    Promote consistent homework completion to strengthen understanding.

    Raise awareness about healthy sleep habits, since rested students perform better.

    Involve parents, especially those with less education, to create supportive home environments.

  
    
    
    Solution 
    1. Identify Struggling Students Early

    Use student records to flag those with low grades, poor attendance, and incomplete homework.

    Create a "Math Support List" and prioritize them for intervention.

2. Offer Targeted Support Programs

    After-school tutoring for weak students.

    Peer learning groups where strong students help others.

    Weekly math problem-solving workshops for practice and confidence-building.

3. Improve Attendance and Homework Completion

    Track and follow up on students with frequent absences.

    Introduce a "Homework Completion Tracker" with small rewards.

    Communicate with parents about the importance of attendance and homework.

4. Promote Healthy Habits (Sleep & Study Time)

    Educate students and parents about the impact of sleep on brain performance.

    Encourage creating study schedules with fixed daily math practice time.

5. Engage Parents More Effectively

    Organize parent meetings to share their child's progress.

    Send simple math tips home for parents to support learning.

    Provide guides for non-educated parents on how to motivate children.

6. Use AI or Technology Tools

    Introduce interactive math apps and platforms that adapt to student levels.

    Provide short online lessons for review at home.

7. Monitor and Adjust Regularly

    Set monthly performance reviews using data (grades, attendance, homework).

    Adjust teaching methods based on what works best for each group.
